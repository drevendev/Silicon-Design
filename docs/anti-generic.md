# Diagnose generic decisions

A familiar pattern can be excellent. The warning is an interchangeable composition whose choices have no relationship to content or audience. Use these as questions, not an automated style blacklist.

| Symptom | Investigate | A useful response | A valid exception |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Every page is a centered hero followed by three cards | Is the task explanation, comparison, reading or operating? | Rebuild the information order using actual evidence and next steps | Three genuinely comparable plans may belong in three cards |
| Every element uses the accent | Which action or state deserves attention? | Give status, navigation and brand expression distinct roles | A deliberately colorful game world can still keep its HUD legible |
| Repeated gradients, glass, glows or huge radii | Do they express material, depth, brand or a state? | Keep meaningful effects and remove attention without information | Light bloom may be part of a space game's visual fiction |
| A fashionable font pair supplies the whole identity | Do structure, voice, imagery and content reinforce it? | Develop a composition and image language connected to the product | A system sans is often the best body face |
| Every content block is a card | Do borders create meaningful grouping? | Use typography, proximity, rows and separators where appropriate | Independent draggable objects benefit from bounded surfaces |
| Beige, serif and muted green replace the previous default | Was the audience or task considered? | Compare directions at the level of hierarchy and composition | These choices can suit a specific publication or brand |
| Everything animates on entry | What state change does the animation explain? | Keep task feedback and purposeful transitions; reduce repetitive reveals | A narrative introduction may use authored motion with a skip path |
| The hero promises “the future” while the product stays invisible | What can a person actually do, and what evidence exists? | Show an actual result and describe the next action | Abstract copy may belong in a deliberate campaign with supporting context |
| A dashboard opens with decorative KPI tiles | Do the numbers change a decision? | Lead with unresolved work, exceptions or comparisons | Decision-relevant metrics may be the core workflow |

## Three practical tests

**Substitution:** replace the name with an unrelated product. If almost nothing needs to change, inspect the content and structure. This is a critique aid, not a requirement to make every control original.

**Reduction:** temporarily remove decorative effects. The hierarchy and task should remain understandable. Restore effects that provide identity, pleasure or useful emphasis.

**Stress:** use long titles, zero results, failures, large values and a narrow viewport. A design that works only with ideal content is unfinished.

## Beyond removing tells

The absence of a cliché is not a positive direction. Inspect these additional failures:

| Symptom | What to investigate | Useful next experiment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A plausible explanation can justify every ordinary choice | Is the reason genuinely specific or written after the fact? | Compare the same argument against an unrelated product; gather better product material |
| A recognizable hero leads to a generic interior | Has the visual idea become a grammar? | Carry it through the primary task and two different states |
| The mascot or illustration never responds to the task | Does it express anything about the product's behavior? | Explore a meaningful state relationship; retain decoration only where it has a role |
| A pleasant first visit becomes tedious repetition | Are tours, motion and praise serving the person now? | Review a tenth visit and remove recurring friction |
| “No items” covers both completion and failure | Does the interface understand why content is absent? | Write and compose those states separately |
| The agent reports tests as proof of taste | Which conclusion did the checks actually establish? | Report operation and character separately, with a design judgment supported by specific observations |

The [source register](../research/sources.md) distinguishes anti-slop heuristics and online opinion from accessibility standards and direct usability evidence. A regex or “AI score” cannot establish visual quality.
