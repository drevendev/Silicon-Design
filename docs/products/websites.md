# Websites

Choose the visitor's journey before a hero pattern. A marketing site needs comprehension and evidence; a publication needs a readable route into its articles; a portfolio needs work a visitor can assess.

- First screen: identify the product or subject, the intended visitor and the useful next step. Show a representative result when it clarifies the promise.
- Structure: order sections by the questions they answer. Use a comparison table for comparable facts, a sequence for a process, and a reading column for an argument. Let a real story create rhythm.
- Identity: combine type, image direction, layout and voice. A distinctive crop or illustration may do more than repeated decorative backgrounds.
- Trust: use actual work, screenshots and attributable evidence. Keep invented testimonials, customer logos, usage counts and performance figures out of product claims.
- Action: make destination and consequence clear. A demo link needs a real demo; a prototype action should be explicitly labelled.
- Delivery: test image loading, font fallback, navigation, responsive content and reasonable asset weight. Decide an asset/performance budget from the deployment context rather than adding costly effects by habit.

Review one first-visit journey through evidence to action, including navigation at a narrow width. A reading site also needs a long article with links, lists, figures and a long title. A large headline alone does not establish editorial quality.
