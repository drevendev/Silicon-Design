## What we adopt from the additional reading

The ideas below are paraphrased methods, not copied skill bodies or code. See the manifest for exact pins and reviewed ranges.

- Anthropic frontend-design: decisive positive direction and avoiding convergence across output. We add retained same-brief proof rather than interpreting a style list as a recipe.
- Impeccable: new-work exploration, critique and composition informed by the actual material. We keep alternatives as rendered artifacts and scale the work to the brief.
- Taste-skill: specific hierarchy, use-size critique and detailed craft. We do not adopt a universal preferred face, default dials or a ban on a particular library.
- UI UX Pro Max: context-aware search and system reasoning. A searchable catalogue can widen the candidate set; a style recommendation is not a design decision or quality result.
- Stitch design-md / loop: an explicit semantic direction, iteration and retained context between surfaces. Tool integrations are optional.
- Vercel: check concrete interface rules and keep composition contracts maintainable. A pointer to remotely changing guidelines is not a pinned body of standards.
- Superdesign: history of retained options and iteration, with caution: the inspected prompt itself carries rounded-card defaults. Historical/unmaintained status limits its use as a current recommendation.
- Agentation: attach review to a located element and state. We independently author a lightweight correction log; no package/code is imported. Its license is not treated as MIT permission.
- Screenshot-to-code: targeted recapture/edit loops and retained versions. Pixel similarity is narrower than content fidelity or design quality.
- Make Real: sketch/annotation as explicit input and preserving the artifact through edits. Its archived status and guessed-style prompts are limitations, not reasons to copy its runtime.

NN/g's bounded prototype evaluations inform our ordinary-task and design-system checks, not a claim about every current model. The Smashing tutorials support better briefs and iterative artifacts; the fictional Kate walkthrough is not a participant study. Editorial arguments about taste and brand craft broaden reference material, not mandatory UI styles. We reject model-generated probability scores as calibrated evidence and simulated users as audience research.
