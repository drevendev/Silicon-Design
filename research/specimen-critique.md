# The first specimens are still too generic

Review date: **2 November 2026**. Trigger: maintainer feedback on the initial preview. The technical checks in [verification](../examples/VERIFICATION.md) remain valid within their scope. They did not evaluate whether the design had enough character. The initial presentation gave those checks too much weight.

This is a design critique of the existing [HTML specimens](../examples/specimen.html) and [briefs](../examples/README.md), not a user study or a claim that a replacement has been built. The HTML has not been redesigned in this revision.

## Field Notes

**Observation:** a paper-like background, serif heading, chapter number and a short text column establish an editorial category. They do little to establish this particular journal. The same arrangement could accommodate almost any lifestyle publication. There is no distinctive field material or authored image language to examine.

**Consequence:** the brief explains readability better than it establishes identity. “Editorial” becomes the design answer before the subject has shaped the composition.

**Revision experiment:** choose a precise kind of observation and introduce genuine material from it: annotated routes, a characteristic recording method, or a relationship between a note and its location. Carry that idea into navigation and reading. Select the material first; do not invent field evidence to make the page feel authentic.

## Dispatch

**Observation:** aligned records and explicit statuses support scanning. The warm accent and system sans do not make the application recognizable. Expandable text demonstrates disclosure, but the prototype stops before resolving work; it cannot show the satisfaction or confidence of finishing the main task.

**Consequence:** this is a reasonable structure exercise. It is weak evidence for the handbook's ambition to create products people care about.

**Revision experiment:** define an actual kind of operational work and examine one case from uncertainty to resolution. Explore an appropriate characteristic detail in comparison, history or completion feedback. Retain useful density and familiar controls. A distinctive ornament attached to a generic queue would leave the underlying problem open.

## Mosslight

**Observation:** the lantern belongs to the crafting subject. Yet the large illustration stays the same after crafting; most of its meaning lives outside the state change. The panel combines a familiar dark-green surface, warm accent, serif title and resource box. The action demonstrates resource subtraction, not a coherent game world or a rewarding loop.

**Consequence:** the motif is relevant but mostly decorative. Theme coherence has been mistaken for a developed product identity.

**Revision experiment:** explore the visible consequence of making light: how the crafted object, workshop or immediate path changes. Connect the world response, resource truth and language. Check repetition, reduced motion and the actual runtime. A larger glow alone would not answer the critique.

## What the next version must demonstrate

- A specific intended feeling grounded in an audience and moment.
- Material and decisions that belong to the imagined product's subject.
- A recognizable idea carried through the main task, a difficult state and a completed action.
- Useful details that remain welcome after repeated use.
- Separate conclusions about operation and character, with evidence and judgment labelled.

This is a revision brief. Design and implementation remain open work; no new screenshots, recall tests or improved attachment are claimed.
